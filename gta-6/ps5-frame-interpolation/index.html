GTA 6 on PS5 targets 30 FPS: free open-source frame interpolation, and its limits

The PS5 still targets 30 frames a second. A Windows PC can draw the pictures in between, and only on its own monitor.

Checked 19 November 2026. SmoovPlay release read that day: v0.3.8.

GTA 6 on a real console has not been run through this path by us, and SmoovPlay has not published a real-PS5 GTA 6 recording. The GTA VI clips on their compare page are Rockstar's trailer, An Extended Look, replayed as a simulated 1080p30 Remote Play stream. The real PS5 recording they show is Gotham Knights. The steps below are the setup in the docs we read on 19 November 2026.

The short answer

Digital Foundry's console comparison, which we read on 19 November 2026, says PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Series S all target 30 fps, and that none of them has a 40 or 60 fps mode. PS5 Pro holds that 30 more steadily. A steadier 30 is still a 30.

The PlayStation Blog post, 10 November 2026, is about preload and a rolling local-midnight launch on 19 November. For PS5 Pro it describes clearer picture quality, through PSSR, and ray-traced shadows. It does not describe a higher frame-rate mode.

The free tool that changes the picture on a monitor is SmoovPlay, an early open-source Windows app. A PS5 with Remote Play on sends a 1080p stream. chiaki-ng, the open-source Remote Play client, receives it. SmoovPlay then draws extra frames on the PC graphics card with RIFE. A 60 Hz monitor can show 60 pictures a second, a 90 Hz monitor 90, and a 120 Hz or faster monitor up to 120. The console is not modified. The game keeps its own response rate. SmoovPlay's own code is MIT. The Remote Play piece depends on chiaki-ng, which is AGPL-3.0.

Who it is for

This runs only when the picture is on a Windows PC.

Worth setting up

  • You have a Windows 10 or 11 PC and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 30, 40, or 50 series card. That is the range SmoovPlay marks as tested.
  • You can play on that PC's monitor, controller paired to the PC, on a screen at 60 Hz or faster.
  • You want smoother motion in a 30 fps game and can accept the stream's delay plus the frame generator's delay.

Leave it

  • You only have a PS5 and a TV. The extra frames never reach a screen plugged into the console.
  • You want the game itself to render or respond at 60. It keeps the 30 fps target Digital Foundry measured.
  • You were counting on AMD, Intel, an Xbox app, or a capture card as the same setup. The project marks those Experimental.

30 real frames, then extra pictures

Four stops. The game is the first. The monitor is only a display.

What the PS5 sends

Real frames at a 30 fps target. Button timing stays the game's own rate, then the stream adds its own wait.

What the monitor shows

60 pictures on a 60 Hz screen, 90 on 90 Hz, and up to 120 only if the screen is 120 Hz or faster. Each extra picture is drawn from the real frames on either side.

What you need

From the SmoovPlay README hardware table, tag v0.3.8. Where the project says Tested or Experimental, that word is kept.

PCWindows 10 or 11, 64-bit. 8 GB of memory. 12 GB free during setup.
Graphics cardNVIDIA GeForce RTX 30, 40 or 50 series, marked Tested. The machines they name are an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER on Windows 10, and a contributor's RTX 5070 Ti on Windows 11. RTX 20 cards and laptops are not tested yet. AMD Radeon and Intel Arc are Experimental, through DirectML. The project says that path is about 1.7 times slower and has not met a real game on a real AMD or Intel card yet.
ConsoleA PS5 with Remote Play turned on. That is the tested path. A capture card, or a game shown inside an app window, is Experimental.
NetworkEthernet for the console and the PC, or 5 GHz or 6 GHz Wi-Fi. A capture card does not need it. The README says a wired link is the smoothest.
ScreenAny refresh rate: 60 Hz gets 60 fps, 90 Hz gets 90, 120 Hz or faster gets up to 120. G-Sync or FreeSync helps, per the README.
Where you sitController on the PC. Eyes on the PC monitor. A TV cabled to the PS5 keeps the console's own picture.
On the PS5Nothing is installed.

The README's Experimental capture-card line names one player's AVerMedia Live Gamer 4K (GC573) as working since 0.3.1, with its sound still waiting on their word as of 0.3.2. Other cards were tried with recordings standing in for a card. The Xbox app has been recognised from a player's report; a real Xbox stream is not confirmed. Use the PS5 Remote Play path if you want the one the project calls tested.

Set it up

Labels below are the ones in the v0.3.8 README and on chiaki-ng's configuration page. If a button on your build has moved, follow the text in the app. v0.3.8 shipped on 19 November 2026, and this is an early release.

  1. On the PS5, open Settings → System → Remote Play and turn on Enable Remote Play. That menu is the one chiaki-ng documents. To wake the console from rest, the same page also lists Settings → System → Power Saving → Features Available in Rest Mode, then Stay Connected to the Internet and Enable Turning on PS5 from Network. Keep the PS5 and the PC on the same home network.
  2. On the Windows PC, download SmoovPlay-Setup.exe only from the official Releases page. The tag we read is v0.3.8. The installer is an early build and is not code-signed, so the browser or Windows may warn you. Keep SmartScreen, antivirus, and the rest of your protection as they are. To check the file, compare its SHA-256 with the hash on that Releases page. The README's PowerShell command is Get-FileHash on the installer. Skip search ads, re-uploads, and any page that asks you to type a PSN password into it.
  3. Run that installer. The project says setup takes 10 to 20 minutes, installs for the current Windows user, and does not request administrator rights. It checks the PC and downloads about 5 GB — Python, PyTorch, and NVIDIA TensorRT — from those projects' own sources. It finishes by picking settings from a short test of your graphics card and display.
  4. In the app, follow Connect your PS5. Turn Remote Play on if it is still off, sign in to PSN inside chiaki-ng, and register the console. The project says this is about five minutes, once. The sign-in stays in that client and on Sony's own page, the same kind of step as Sony's Remote Play app. BuffForge never asks for the password, the account ID, or a redirect link. If the client asks for a Remote Play code, the console shows one at Settings → System → Remote Play → Link Device. Enter the code in the client. When the screens diverge from these labels, use chiaki-ng's configuration page.
  5. Pair the controller with the PC the way that controller usually pairs. You play looking at the PC monitor. The television plugged into the PS5 keeps showing the console's own picture.
  6. Press Play. In the video window the README lists M for the live menu, F for fullscreen (called the lowest delay), I to turn the extra frames off and on, L for the delay test, and Esc or Q to stop. As of v0.3.8, Capture card resolution stays grey unless the source really is a capture card, because PS5 Remote Play is 1080p at most. Minimise chiaki-ng stays grey unless PS5 Remote Play is the source. A grey 1440p or 4K choice is not a higher Remote Play stream.

Keys for the video window, from the README. The controller plays the game. The keyboard runs SmoovPlay.

MMenu: every setting, live.
FFullscreen. The README calls this the lowest delay.
IExtra frames off and on, so you can compare.
LDelay test for PS5 Remote Play.
Esc or QStop.

On the Experimental app-window path the other app keeps the keyboard, and the README moves these shortcuts to Ctrl+Alt. An update from inside the app has to carry SmoovPlay's signature before it runs. From 0.3.1, Check for updates sits in the launcher side bar.

A starting point, not a tuned profile

Leave the choices the installer just made. Auto-detect on the Settings page runs the short test again after you change monitors. Nothing in this table is a GTA 6 setting we measured. We have not played the game through this path.

OutputKeep Auto-detect's choice. 60 Hz shows 60, 90 Hz shows 90. Up to 120 needs a 120 Hz or faster screen. The README says 60 and 90 are what it has shown and measured live. 120 draws three in-between frames the same way, and no 120 fps clip or live measurement is published yet.
Uneven 30If a locked 30 fps game looks uneven, set Settings → Smoothness → Game frame rate to 30 fps. On Auto, the project says that since 0.3.4 it usually reads a wired 30 fps stream from the picture, and that a still or dark scene can still fool it.
One generatorWhile SmoovPlay and chiaki-ng are running, leave NVIDIA Smooth Motion, AMD Fluid Motion Frames, and Lossless Scaling off. The README lists that as a conflict. Quitting this app leaves your other games alone.
ModelStay on RIFE. That is the model this project runs by default. NVIDIA's Video Frame Generation is optional, not included, and only offered on RTX 40 and 50 after you accept NVIDIA's licence.

chiaki-ng carries the stream

chiaki-ng receives Remote Play, plays the sound, and sends your buttons back. SmoovPlay's how-it-works page calls that the transport: a small frame tap hands each decoded frame across, and SmoovPlay is where the extra frames are drawn. chiaki-ng's own docs describe Remote Play setup. They do not describe a frame-generation mode. On its own, chiaki-ng shows the stream the console sent.

SmoovPlay's NOTICE at tag v0.3.8 says SmoovPlay's own code is MIT (LICENSE), and the patched chiaki-ng remains a separate program under the GNU AGPL 3.0. For this path, Connect your PS5 inside SmoovPlay is the setup. A second installer should not be required. If you do install chiaki-ng by itself, use only its repository and its releases. The old streetpea/chiaki-ng address redirects there.

Delay, picture, and fixes from the docs

SmoovPlay's own timing in the v0.3.8 README, not a measurement of ours and not a GTA 6 session: about 36 ms at a 90 fps output and about 20 ms at 60, on top of Remote Play. In two live Ethernet sessions of a 30 fps PS5 game the medians were 35.4 ms and 35.8 ms. The 60 fps figure is from a recorded clip. About 22 ms of that is the wait while the in-between frames are shown. Press L for the project's delay test, which times a button all the way to the screen. The project points the result at driving, exploring, and story play.

The stream arrives at 1080p, so the picture is a little softer than HDMI straight from the console. That 1080p is the Remote Play limit this project documents. It is not a measurement of the resolution the game renders at.

HUD text over fast motion, very fast camera turns, and thin objects crossing each other can wobble. Press I to compare with the extra frames off. chiaki-ng holds the sound back by as long as the picture spends in SmoovPlay, so the two stay in step.

A game already running at 60 passes through at 60, because there are no gaps to fill. A 30 fps game that dips freezes for a moment and jumps at each dip. Dip smoothing is Experimental, off by default, and the README says it adds about 17 to 50 ms and more graphics work, only while the game is dipping. This page is not claiming that GTA 6 dips, and it leaves Digital Foundry's specific drop counts out.

On Wi-Fi, frames can arrive late and in bunches, which shows up as a hitch however fast the PC is. The README's advice is to plug the PS5 and the PC into the router.

A black picture, or the PS5 saying the content can't be shown: Settings → System → HDMI, turn off Enable HDCP, connect again, and turn HDCP back on before video apps such as Netflix. If it is still black, press Q, then Help → Copy diagnostics, and paste that into a SmoovPlay issue. Send that paste to the project, not to this site.

Questions

Does GTA 6 on PS5 render at 60 FPS with this?

No. Digital Foundry found a 30 fps target and no 40 or 60 mode. SmoovPlay draws extra pictures on the PC between frames the console already sent. The game still renders and responds at its own rate.

I only have a PS5 and a TV. Does any of this apply?

No. The extra frames are drawn on a Windows PC, and you watch them on that PC's monitor, with the controller paired to the PC. A TV plugged into the PS5 shows the console's own picture.

Is chiaki-ng another way to raise the frame rate?

No. chiaki-ng is the Remote Play client. It carries the stream, the sound, and your buttons. It does not generate frames. Without SmoovPlay you are watching the 1080p stream the console sent.

Has this been tried on a real PS5 copy of GTA 6?

Not by us. SmoovPlay's public GTA VI clips are Rockstar's trailer, An Extended Look, played as a simulated 1080p30 stream. The real PS5 recording on their compare page is Gotham Knights.

Will the controls feel faster?

The project says no. Motion looks smoother, and the game still responds like 30 fps, a little later than Remote Play alone. Their note points this at driving, exploring, and story play rather than competitive matches.

Where is the real download, and is it free?

SmoovPlay's own code is MIT. The project says the app is free, with no account, no ads, and no tracking, and that it only talks to GitHub for updates. chiaki-ng is AGPL-3.0. The Windows installer is an early unsigned build. Download SmoovPlay only from its GitHub Releases, and chiaki-ng only from its own repository and releases.
