3. Deep Dive: Duelist & Shadow Gameplay, Weapons & Mechanics

Both new 1.0 classes introduce weapon classes and active combat systems that were completely absent throughout Early Access. Rather than playing like static stat sticks, Swords, Daggers, and Traps are built around kinetic stance-swapping, positional invisibility, and combo pay-offs (Maxroll; EverythingEdinburgh).

Duelist: Swords, Stances & Companions

Weapon: Swords Stances: Ready / Sand / Blood Knight & Weapon Master

The Duelist is Path of Exile 2's first dedicated sword specialist. Gameplay revolves around weaving rapid slashes and bleed stacks while shifting between three active Stances—Ready, Sand, and Blood—to tilt your kit toward mobility, evasion/defense, or raw bleed damage.

  • Recruit a Squire (Ascendancy Notable): Summons a persistent Squire companion that you can outfit directly with regular looted items and any melee weapon of your choice—acting as a gear-scaling partner rather than a disposable minion.
  • Ethereal Chariot & Warbanner: Vault onto a spectral Chariot, charge straight through packs, and slam into the first target to plant your chosen Warbanner at the impact zone for immediate offensive buffs.
  • Ascendancy Timing Note: Knight is confirmed for Day 1 of 1.0, while some press releases noted Master-at-Arms/Weapon Master timing details—verify final patch notes one week before December 11 (ExileClasses).

Shadow: Daggers, Traps & Kill-Reset Stealth

Daggers & 8 Trap Types Mechanic: Smoke Stealth Assassin & Saboteur

Unveiled as the surprise tenth class at ExileCon 2026, the Shadow pairs high-tempo Dagger combat with Traps as a brand-new standalone weapon category (8 trap types). Early hands-on playtests already rate Shadow as one of the fastest and most fluid classes in the game.

  • High-Tempo Stealth: Trigger a short smoke dash to become completely invisible to monsters outside detection range. Crucially, the dash cooldown resets on every kill, letting you weave in and out of stealth between every pack to trigger out-of-stealth damage bonuses without slowing down clear speed.
  • Blade Impale & Extraction: Stick daggers into enemies and rip them out for secondary bursts. Skills like Blade Vortex pull all nearby embedded blades into a high-speed spinning ring around your character.
  • Ascendancy Choice: Pick Assassin for critical/dagger execution or Saboteur (Infiltrator) to specialize in trap chains and stealth setups.

5. Conquerors of Wraeclast: Full League Loop, Castles & Reforging Bench

Conquerors of Wraeclast is the 1.0 challenge league launching alongside the full release. It merges tactical army battles, faction territory control across a shared Atlas map, automated Commander mapping, and a powerful new fracturing bench (Maxroll).

Step-by-Step Progression Loop

  • Campaign Start (Rescue Varek): Free Varek in Clearfell and liberate his soldiers from nearby camps. Collect Gold and a new resource called Supplies from monster-guarded chests to field larger armies, plus explore 17 new Points of Interest where special rare monsters unlock unique troop types.
  • Tactical Board + Live Combat: Before each battle, counter enemy formations (for example, placing arrow-resistant units on archer lanes). Once combat starts, the camera zooms in and you fight directly alongside your army to defeat the enemy Commander for loot keys.
  • Endgame Faction & Castle Building: Meet Varek east of your Atlas starting area and pledge to one of three factions—Karui, Ezomyte, or Maraketh. Your stronghold visually evolves from humble huts into a sprawling castle that automatically trains troops over time as long as you fund Gold upkeep.
  • 100 Shared Atlas Castles: Team up with every player in your faction across the entire league to siege 100 contested castles. Holding castles grants personal and faction-wide Item Rarity buffs, instant Atlas fast-travel, and lucrative castle maps (including merged-monster encounters and Vampire Wisps). Anti-snowball scaling raises siege difficulty as one faction holds more castles.
  • Commanders Run Maps While You Sleep: Unlock Commanders like Athela, High Justicar, assign idle castle troops, queue multiple maps in a row, clear fog-of-war towers, and slot up to 3 Tablets to juice loot—then collect the spoils from a reward window.

Reforging Bench & Multi-Fractured Crafting

Reward chests drop new Runeforging Currencies that let you select one or more modifiers on an item and turn them into Forgemarked mods. Placing the item into the Reforging Bench rerolls every Forgemarked modifier into a new stat and Fractures all of them simultaneously—enabling multi-fractured crafting bases for the first time (fractured items cannot be Forgemarked again). Targeted endgame currencies like the Bloodvial of Rithva force the resulting fractured roll into a specific modifier group.

7. Early Access Wipe Rules, Free-to-Play Model & Stash Tab Advice

Two of the highest-stakes questions ahead of December 11 are whether Early Access progress carries forward and what Free-to-Play newcomers actually need to spend. Here are the verified rules from GGG's FAQ (Timesaver; EB24; PoE Vault):

What Carries Into 1.0 (Account-Wide)

  • All purchased Stash Tabs (usable immediately on Day 1)
  • All Cosmetics / MTX & unspent Store Points (EA packs came with 300 points)
  • Shared PoE 1 & PoE 2 account-wide MTX (PC & Xbox; see PlayStation exception below)
  • Free 1.0 Launch Registration Cosmetics (emailed after launch if registered by Dec 11)

What Stays Frozen in Early Access

  • All EA characters (0.1–0.5.5 Forbidden Rites) move to a permanent isolated Early Access league
  • EA characters can never enter 1.0 Standard or Conquerors of Wraeclast
  • All hoarded EA currency (Divines, Exalts, Gold) and gear stay behind—1.0 starts from zero
  • Early Access Supporter Packs retire permanently before launch, replaced by 5 new 1.0 packs

Free-to-Play Guide: Stash Tabs & PlayStation Caveat

Every new Free-to-Play account receives the entire six-act campaign, all 10 classes, and the full Conquerors of Wraeclast endgame for $0, starting with 4 regular stash tabs (Timesaver F2P breakdown). Those 4 tabs are plenty to clear the campaign and early maps. If you reach endgame crafting and trading, the two highest-value optional upgrades are a Currency Tab and a Merchant's / Premium Tab.

Important PlayStation Warning: While store points and cosmetics transfer between Path of Exile 1 and Path of Exile 2 across PC and Xbox, PlayStation purchases stay locked to PlayStation—and cosmetics purchased on PC or Xbox cannot be equipped on PlayStation.

9. Frequently Asked Questions (PoE 2 1.0 Launch)

Is Path of Exile 2 entering Early Access in December 2026?

No—Early Access started on December 6, 2024. December 11, 2026 (PST) is when Path of Exile 2 leaves Early Access and launches its Free-to-Play 1.0 full release.

Do Early Access characters or Divine Orbs transfer to 1.0 Standard?

No. Characters are not deleted, but they move to a permanent isolated Early Access league and can never enter 1.0 Standard or Conquerors of Wraeclast. Only account-wide purchases (stash tabs, cosmetics, store points) carry over.

Should I buy an Early Access Supporter Pack now or wait for December 11?

Wait if you only want to play the finished 1.0 game for free. Buying before launch only makes sense if you want to play 0.5.5 right away, want the retiring Early Access-exclusive cosmetics before they leave the store permanently, or plan to spend $30 on 300 store points for stash tabs anyway.

Which 2 of the 12 planned classes are still coming after 1.0?

GGG confirmed 10 of 12 planned classes ship at 1.0 launch (8 Early Access classes plus Duelist and Shadow), with the remaining two classes and Druid's third Ascendancy arriving in post-1.0 updates.

How many free stash tabs do Free-to-Play accounts start with?

Four regular stash tabs—enough to finish the six-act campaign and early maps. Heavy traders and crafters typically add a Currency Tab and Merchant's/Premium Tab later.

When do the full Path of Exile 2 1.0 patch notes release?

Per Maxroll's ExileCon 2026 coverage, GGG plans to publish the complete 1.0 patch notes roughly one week before the December 11 launch.
